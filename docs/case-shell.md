# The case-study shell

Shared by `finsynth.html` and `case.html` (and, as generated copies, `locked.html` and
`tools/locked.template.html`): the rails, the story-tab band, the back circle, the
masthead facts grid, the section separator, and the chapter heads.

Split out of `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-08-24; the text is unchanged, so a **bold cross-reference**
in it may name a block that now lives in a sibling file under `docs/`. `CLAUDE.md` itself
keeps the commands, the file map, the type scale and the hard rules.

**Section rail:** `finsynth.html` only (`.index`, fixed in the **top-left corner of the viewport** at `top: 168px; left: 32px` — top-aligned, not vertically centred, and anchored to the window rather than computed off `--content`/`--gutter`, so it stays parked while the column recentres; hidden below 1120px where the margin can't hold it) — `case.html` has a different rail, the tick rail below. No header and no numbers: one `<a>` per chapter hanging off a **continuous hairline rail** (`ol { border-left: 1px solid var(--line) }`), with the active chapter's segment inked in — `a::before` is a 1px bar at `left: -1px` spanning the row, `--text`, `scaleY(0)` → `scaleY(1)` for `aria-current='true'` on `.28s cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)`. It is `scaleY` from a top origin rather than an animated `height` so a chapter change stays on the compositor and can never reflow the rail. Inactive labels are `--muted` (not `--faint` — an inactive item still has to be readable) and go `--text` on hover. `li.sub` (the three decisions inside 07) steps only the *label* in; the inked segment stays on the one shared rail line. An earlier version replaced the rail with a per-chapter horizontal tick under a `Table of Contents` header — reverted on purpose; don't bring it back. `aria-current` is set by the scroll tracker (the same one that broadcasts `asksection` to the chat), so the rail and the suggestion chips always agree on which chapter you are in.

**The rail sits at 168px, and it was written at 92 (2026-08-23).** 92 cleared the 81px header and nothing else, which was the whole story until the story-tab band went full width: the band sticks flush under the header and now runs the full viewport, so it passes *beneath* the rail and ends at 148. The rail lives outside `main`'s stacking context (`main { position: relative; z-index: 1 }`, `.index` is a sibling at z-index 50), so it always paints **above** the band — which meant the band's bottom shadow drew a faint line straight through the first two labels. Two other fixes were considered and rejected: the `rail-peek` `--bg` halo idiom would notch the band's shadow rather than hide the collision, and dropping the band's shadow would take away its only separation from the content scrolling under it. So the rail clears the band the same way it always cleared the header, one surface later: 148 + 20. Retune `.story-tab-sticky`'s `top` or padding and this number follows it. **That sibling relationship is load-bearing, and the vault has to rebuild it by hand (2026-08-24):** the fragment is injected inside `main`, so `sectionIndex()` in `tools/locked.template.html` moves the rail back out to `main`'s parent before arming the scroll spy. Leave it nested and the rail inherits `main`'s stacking context, where `z-index: 50` cannot beat a band at `55` no matter what the numbers say. See `docs/vault.md`.

**The shadow that forced that move is gone, and 168 stays** — the band is still 67px of opaque `--bg` parked at 81, so a rail at 92 would have its first two labels *behind* a bar rather than merely crossed by a hairline. The collision the shadow caused was the symptom; the band passing beneath the rail is the cause, and only the cause sets the number.

**Opening story tabs (`finsynth.html`):** The four opening chapters — 01 The analyst, 02 The problem, 03 The product, 04 The limitation — share one sticky navigator (`.story-tab-sticky` → `.story-tablist` → four `.story-tab`s) above them, and each chapter carries `class="story-tab-panel" role="tabpanel"`. **It is a navigator, not a real tab set:** nothing is ever hidden, the four chapters stay in one continuous scroll sequence, and a click just `scrollIntoView`s its panel. So the reader who scrolls past it never loses a chapter, and the reader who wants to jump gets four addresses in one control. The driver (its own IIFE, next to the ask-cat one) sets `aria-selected` from an `IntersectionObserver` on the four panels (`rootMargin: '-120px 0px -55% 0px'`), so the band tracks the scroll the way `.index` does; a click also `history.replaceState`s the hash, and the four `.index` links to those chapters are intercepted to run through the same `activate()` so the rail and the band can never disagree. `.story-tab-panel { scroll-margin-top: 168px }` is what keeps a landing clear of the header plus the band — it beats `.chapter`'s own 76px on source order, not specificity, so don't move either rule above the other. Measured: every one of the four tabs lands its chapter at `top: 158` against a band bottom of 148. The 10px is `.reveal`'s `translateY(10px)`, still in flight when `scrollIntoView` measures the box; the clearance is what matters and it is positive at every tab.

**The band is full width, and it was column width (Ritika, 2026-08-23: "this needs full width.. but also still its not fully visiblt so lower a bit").** Two complaints in one sentence, and the second is what forced the first: the band was a 660px strip sitting on the reading column, so it read as a floating pill rather than as a second bar, and at `top: 0` it tucked under the sticky header. It is full-bleed now with **`.wf`'s own idiom** — `margin-inline: calc(50% - 50vw)` against `width: auto`, with the gutter restated as padding — so it grows to exactly 100vw without ever naming `100vw` (`docScrollX` stays 0 at 1698, 1440 and 390), and it sticks at **`top: 81px`**, the header's height. Nothing new was drawn: the ground is `--bg` and the only addition was a bottom shadow, the band's separation from the content scrolling beneath it. That shadow is also what moved the chapter rail down — see **Section rail** above.

**There is no shadow now, stuck or not (Ritika, 2026-08-23: "dont already have a shadow and all.. only when it sticks to top", then "no need of shadow").** Two notes, and the first one was implemented before the second arrived: the shadow moved onto an `.is-stuck` class that a scroll-and-resize watcher toggled, so a band sitting in flow above the chapters was flat and a band parked under the header lifted. Her second note declines the lift altogether, and the band does not need it — its ground is `--bg` and fully opaque, so nothing shows through it, and the blue active segment is what says *this is a bar*. What went is a hairline of depth, not the bar. **The class went with the shadow**, and so did its watcher, its `transition`, and its `prefers-reduced-motion` override: nothing in the file reads `.is-stuck` now, and re-adding a stuck state means re-adding all four.

**The active segment is filled blue and set bold (Ritika, same round: "switch colour need to be \" blue\" and highlight or bold the text").** It was `--bg-raised` under `--text` ink, which is the quiet lift `.evo-tabs` uses, and in a full-width band that quiet was the problem: nothing said which of the four you were reading. `--blue-ink` is the blue this page already owns, so no token was added. The label takes **`--bg`, not a fixed white**, so it stays legible on both of that token's values (near-white on `#345bc3` in light, near-black on `#7f9dff` in dark). And bold costs no width here because `--mono` is a fixed-pitch stack: 500 → 700 cannot shift the four segments as the selection moves (measured `[139, 139, 139, 165]`, identical through a full cycle of all four tabs in both themes).

**03's head is one line, and it was two (Ritika, same round, on `#start h2`: "why 2 lines?").** It read `FinSynth already solved parts of the analyst's job.` — 677px against the 660px column, so it wrapped by 17px and was the only `h2` on the page that did. **`analyst's` is the word that went, not `already`:** chapters 01 and 02 are about the analyst and the tab above the head says so, while `already` is the word that sets up 04's limitation. One line at 1698 and 1440 (38px tall).

**Tick rail (`case.html`):** A chapter-progress rail on the **right** (`.case-rail`, fixed at `top: 50%; right: 32px`, `translateY(-50%)`) — one 34px tick per chapter, the one you're in inked and at full length (Ritika, 2026-08-22, from a reference image of right-aligned dashes with the last one bolder). Built by its own IIFE after the renderer, since the chapters it indexes only exist by then: `<li><a href="#ch-N"><i></i></a></li>` per `main .chapter[id]` — the renderer emits `id="ch-<n>"` for exactly this.

**Ticks, not labels, and that is the difference from finsynth's `.index`.** A short case study's chapter names are two words each (`Context`, `What I worked on`, `What I learned`), so a 168px labelled list in the margin would out-weigh the three-section page it indexes; the tick says *where you are* and nothing else. It is **not** the per-chapter horizontal tick that was tried on `finsynth.html` and reverted — that one replaced a labelled rail that was working, under a `Table of Contents` header; this page never had a rail at all. And it is **vertically centred** rather than parked in a corner like `.index`: with no labels there is no reading order to align to the masthead, so the rail sits where the eye is. Icon-only links, so each `<a>` carries both `title` and `aria-label` with the chapter's `h2` text (through `esc()`, which escapes `& < > "`) — same rule as the footer marks.

The tick keeps **one box in every state and only ever scales**: `2px` tall at `scaleX(.6) scaleY(.5)` inactive → `transform: none` for `aria-current='true'`, `transform-origin: 100% 50%` so it grows leftward, away from the page edge. Transform-only for the same reason `.index` animates `scaleY` and not `height` — a chapter change can never reflow the rail. `scaleY(.5)` on a 2px bar lands on a crisp 1px. Inactive is `--line-hover` because `case.html` has no `--line-strong` token; `--faint` on hover, `--text` active.

`aria-current` uses the **same test as `.index`** — the last chapter whose top has crossed 42% of the viewport, plus a bottom-of-page pin because the closing chapter is short — rAF-throttled on scroll and resize, so the two shells agree on what "current chapter" means. The nav ships `hidden` and empty and is only unhidden after a successful build, so **"Case not found" and any single-chapter case get no rail** (`targets.length < 2`: one tick is a dot, not a position gauge). It fades out with `pointer-events: none` under `body.chat-open:not(.chat-popped)` — the docked drawer owns the right edge; a popped-out window leaves it alone. Hidden below **1024px** (not `.index`'s 1120px — 34px of tick needs far less margin than a labelled list), and reduced motion drops the transitions and the fade-in but keeps the ticks. `finsynth.html` has `.index` and none of this.

**Section separator (`finsynth.html` / `case.html`):** A hairline above each top-level section (Ritika, 2026-08-22: "a seperator of each section"). No new pattern — `index.html` already divides its rows with exactly this rule; this applies it to the case-study shell. The gap is split unevenly around it, **40px above / 56px below** (28/44 under 720px), because the rule introduces the section beneath it rather than closing the one above.

It is keyed on the **numbered tag**, not on `.chapter` — `section:has(> .chapter-head > .chapter-num)` — and both halves of that matter. (It carried `.credits` by name until the Team section was removed on 2026-08-22; the selector is one clause again.) `.chapter` would miss **08, which is `.close`**, and it would wrongly catch the three `.decision-num` sub-sections inside 07 (and, until 2026-08-23, the unlabelled `.evo` interstitial, since folded into 05 — see **The switcher moved into 05**): a full-width rule above those flattens them into peers of the chapters. They keep the tighter gap and no rule, and that contrast is what marks the nested level. **Pill = new chapter = rule.** So don't give a nested section a `.chapter-num`, and don't switch this to a `.chapter` selector.

`:has()` is doing the job a sibling selector normally would, because each chapter sits in its own `.wrap` and chapters are therefore **not siblings** — `+` and `:first-of-type` cannot reach across them. Without `:has()` support the rules simply don't draw and nothing else shifts. Two related cleanups came with it: `.chapter:first-of-type { padding-top: 88px }` was **dead in `finsynth.html`** (one section per wrap means it matched *every* chapter, so its 88px was the real value everywhere and the 76px below it never applied) and is gone, folded into one number; and **`case.html`'s renderer was flipped to `<div class="wrap"><section class="chapter">`** — wrap outside section, matching finsynth. With the wrap inside, the border drew on the section and spanned the full page width, landing 24px past the text on each side instead of flush with the 660px column. Nothing in that file couples `.chapter` to `.wrap`, so the flip was isolated; verified rendering, every chapter ruled, rule width exactly 660px.

**Chapter heads (`finsynth.html` / `case.html`):** four parts, in order — `.chapter-num` tag → `h2` → `.chapter-gloss` lede → optional `.chapter-key` callout. Structure borrowed from a reference Ritika picked (2026-08-22), translated into this page's language rather than copied: monochrome throughout, and the `.chapter-num` tag stays a hairline outline rather than the reference's tinted chip. The **one filled surface is `.chapter-key`**, and that is a later decision (2026-08-22) from a second reference — see below.

- `.chapter-num` is a **tag**, not a line of text — inline-block, 11px mono caps in `--muted`, `1px solid var(--line)`, `border-radius: 999px`. Bounding it binds label and title into one object instead of two stacked lines. Only top-level chapters get the pill; `.decision-num` inside 07 stays a bare label, and that is what marks it as the nested level — don't pill it.
- `.chapter-gloss` is a **lede, not a caption**: `--t-body-lg` in `--muted`, the same size as the body copy that follows it and separated from it by ink alone. It used to be `--t-body-md` — *smaller* than the body — which stacked four type sizes in the head's first four lines and made the summary read as subordinate to the paragraph it summarises.
- `.chapter-key` is the chapter's one point, in a **soft tinted panel**: a tinted ground, 14px radius, `22px 24px` padding, **no border**, holding a mono micro-caps `.k` label over a `--t-body-lg` / weight-500 line in `--text`. It was a 2px `--line-hover` **left rule** until 2026-08-22, when Ritika pointed at a reference panel and asked for "this kind of div" — as a filled block it reads as the thing the chapter resolves to, where the rule read as an aside running alongside the prose. **This is not a new material:** `.canvas` and `.term` already ground content-column surfaces in `--bg-raised`, and 14px is the radius `.card` uses on the portfolio, so the panel is assembled entirely from what the page already owns — which is why it needed no new token and flips with the theme for free. **The tinted token is `--pain-bg` in both shells** (`#f0efff` / `#242231`, a violet-tinged tint declared alongside `--bg-raised`). It was a real drift for a day: `finsynth.html` had settled on `--pain-bg` for `.chapter-key` and `.nav-back` while `case.html` used `--bg-raised` for both and carried no `--pain-bg` at all. `case.html` took the token and the panel on 2026-08-23, so the two files now draw the same panel on the same ground. **`.nav-back` is the one rule left in that drift** — `--pain-bg` on finsynth against `--bg-raised` on `case.html` — and it stays there on purpose: it is a visible 36px control Ritika has not spoken about, so reconciling it is her call rather than something to change quietly under a parity argument. It is **borderless on purpose**, unlike those two: they carry a hairline because they contain artwork and code that need bounding, while this holds one line of the page's own voice and the tint alone lifts it. Padding drops to `18px` and the radius to 12px below 560px, where 48px of horizontal padding would eat a phone measure. Lede muted, key in ink — the head still carries four levels on **two type sizes**, because colour, the tag and now the panel do the rest. That remains the rule: if a head needs another distinction, reach for ink or ground, not another size.

**The panel was stripped from every key and then restored, and the round trip is the entry (2026-08-23).** Two annotations in one batch asked for the box to come off two keys — 04's, *"no need to keep it in. a div"*, and the turning point's, *"no need it to be in div"* — and they were read as a verdict on the object rather than on those two placements, so `.chapter-key` lost its ground, its radius and its padding site-wide and became a label over a line. So **the panel is the treatment and the two keys she named are the exceptions** — which is the only reading that satisfies both notes, and the lesson worth keeping is the general one: two same-day notes on two instances of one pattern are notes about those instances, not about the pattern.

**The opt-out was a `.plain` class and it lasted a few hours.** `.chapter-key.plain { padding: 0; border-radius: 0; background: none }` un-tinted exactly the two keys she had named, and its two declarations were picked for specificity rather than for looks: no `margin`, because `.turning-point`'s `margin-top: 0` was a single-class selector (0,1,0) and a `margin` shorthand at (0,2,0) would have outranked it and put the section's own padding back above the panel that opened the section; and `padding: 0` at (0,2,0) so it beat the `@media (max-width: 560px)` step's `.chapter-key { padding: 18px }`, which comes later in source and would otherwise have drawn padding on a phone with no ground under it. It is **gone**, because both elements it existed for stopped being keys later the same day — 04's is body prose and the turning point is deleted (see **Three keys stopped being keys**) — so the panel is now on every element that is still a key and there is nothing to opt out. Worth keeping for the specificity reasoning, which is what any future variant of this panel will have to solve again. **`case.html` took the restore and then the deletion**, on 2026-08-23, together with the `--pain-bg` token and the 560px step: its renderer emits no key at all, so nothing there renders differently in any of the three states, but the shared shell is supposed to be identical in the two files and it had been left holding first the stripped version and then the `.plain` rule finsynth had already deleted. The lesson is the keep-the-two-in-sync rule read the other way round: **a rule finsynth deletes has to be deleted here too**, not just added here when finsynth adds one.
